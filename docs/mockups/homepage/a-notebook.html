A touch deck for your desktop that changes with the app you're using.

deckd runs a small daemon on your computer and serves a page of buttons to any browser — your phone, an old tablet, a second laptop. Focus Firefox and you get tab controls. Open Gmail and you get compose, reply and archive. No app to install on the phone, no USB hardware.

How it works

  1. A small program on your computer watches which window has focus.
  2. It picks the deck for that app — or for the website you're on.
  3. Your phone redraws instantly. Presses come back as keystrokes, commands, or media controls.

Websites are apps too

Most remotes see "Firefox" and stop there. deckd reads the window title, so Gmail, YouTube and Google Meet each get their own buttons, driving the site's own keyboard shortcuts. No browser extension, and any site with shortcuts can have a deck.

Everything that's playing

A music app, a podcast in a browser tab, a video in mpv: every player shows up in one list with play, pause and skip, a tap away from any deck.

Your phone as a trackpad

For everything a deck doesn't cover: drag to move the pointer, tap to click, and a keyboard when you need to type.

Animation. The phone is the real client; the window on the right stands in for your desktop.

A scroll wheel for any window

The strip on the right edge of every deck scrolls whatever's focused — flick it and it coasts, like a real wheel.

Animation.

Decks for anything

Open an app, run a command, open a site, lock the screen — beside live CPU, memory, swap and disk. Decks are built in the editor inside the app, and every screen updates as you save.
